# Territories: cstack self-imagery

/creative-direction on [the brief](../../briefs/2026-10-03-self-imagery.creative-brief.yaml). The owner asked for no approvals in this run, so the recommendation below was taken as the decision; it stays reversible in one file (`work/figures/make-figures.py`).

**Objective.** Keep the clarity of the Swiss poster and add one thing the current figures lack: something alive. **Gold standard.** A studio would spend a week drawing the figure by hand, test it at stamp size and at poster size, and cut it if it ever needed a caption to be funny.

**Reference mechanism, by idea only.** The owner's photograph is internal reference; nothing is traced, quoted or used as input. What transfers: play standing in for creativity, a floor of positions standing in for possibilities, one living figure mid-move in a calm, empty studio, and deadpan good humour. What does not: the animal, the game mat, its dots, its four colours, its lettering, the camera angle.

## A. The field and the figure (recommended)

- **Idea.** Discipline is the floor you play on.
- **Tension it resolves.** cstack sells restraint in a category that sells abundance; restraint reads as joyless until something plays on it.
- **Product truth.** cstack is a set of public positions (skills, flows, checkers, canon, a workspace) and a job uses a few of them. The square is already the unit.
- **Mechanism.** A grid of square positions, outlined where nothing stands and solid where something does. A figure built from the same square (a bar body five units long, one square head turned 17 degrees, limbs a quarter unit wide, no tail) stands on three positions and reaches for a fourth, mid-move. Distance: far (a photograph of play, carried into a type-and-grid poster).
- **Behaviour.** The figure appears only in poster moments (the hero, a social card). Diagrams that must be read use the field and the transit line, never the figure.
- **Art direction.** Klein blue and white on the poster; warm white, ink and Klein blue in diagrams. No shading, no perspective, no second colour.
- **Refuses.** Circles in coloured rows, an animal drawn from life, a mascot with a face, a wink in the copy.

## B. The stretch

- **Idea.** The wordmark itself bends to reach a position.
- **Critique.** Breaks R-MARK-01 (the wordmark is never altered). A logo that performs tricks becomes a gimmick within a week. Cut.

## C. The empty studio

- **Idea.** A vast warm ground with one Klein blue square set down slightly off the grid, as if someone just left it there.
- **Critique.** Beautiful and on brand, but it is the current system with less in it. It answers "calmer", not "more inspiring". Kept as the runner-up for a future social card.

## Critique of A

| Criterion | Reading |
|---|---|
| Brand fit | One colour, the square as the only unit, flush-left grid, wordmark untouched. |
| Product truth | The field diagram is generated from the skill map in `docs/architecture.md`, so its squares are the real skills by stage. |
| Distinctiveness | No AI tool home uses a figure built from its own grid unit. |
| Risk | A geometric quadruped can read as clip art. Mitigation: one figure, small, never explained, never in a diagram; built from the unit so it reads as typography, not illustration. |
| Generic moves named | A mascot with eyes, a speech bubble, confetti. None used. |

## Mutation

Pushed with the transit-map mechanism already in the system: in the docs diagram the field becomes a station board, and one job's route runs through it, stopping only where it needs to. The row it skips says "not this time". Before: a table of skill names. After: a field of positions with one honest route through it, the route this job took.

## Decision

A, with the mutation. The hero keeps its poster, text and wordmark; the row of ten squares with one turned becomes a row of twelve positions, one per column, and the turned square becomes the figure's head. The drift and nine-stop figures stay as they are. A new diagram, `docs/images/the-field.svg`, lives in `docs/architecture.md`, not in the README.

## Rejection list

- No circles in rows; no second, third or fourth colour; no animal drawn from life or from any photograph.
- No figure in a diagram that has to be read.
- No more than one figure per image; no figure standing on more than three positions.
- No wordmark that moves, bends or loses its full stop.
- No practitioner's name, and no name of a game or toy, inside any image.
