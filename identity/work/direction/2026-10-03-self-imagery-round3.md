# Round 3: the square as the symbol, posters in the Swiss register

/creative-direction on [the brief](../../briefs/2026-10-03-self-imagery.creative-brief.yaml), after the owner's verdicts on round 1 (the hand-drawn figure: "this sucks") and round 2 (two generated heroes: "I don't like any").

**What the owner asked for (22:43Z).** The original photograph used somewhere, credited. The rest as Swiss, Vignelli-register posters. The blue square as cstack's symbol. Generated media or vector work, whichever the poster needs. Taste bar: his canon in photography, which the generated pictures so far do not reach.

**Objective.** A poster set, not a hero: one system that gives the README, the docs pages and a social card the same voice, where the Klein blue square does the work the figure and the cube were trying to do.

## Method, before making

From the canon entries cstack already holds (vignelli, swiss-grid-systems, bureau-borsche), the mechanisms that transfer:

- one typeface, few sizes, hierarchy from position, weight and space (here: FreeSans, three sizes, 300 / 64 / 44)
- the grid derived from the type and treated as the only legal positions (twelve columns, margin 48, every square sits on a column)
- a fixed frame with the content remade each time (the band, the wordmark, the square are fixed; the move changes)
- one strong move per poster; tell the whole story with very few elements
- black and one colour on warm white; the colour is spent on one thing

Rejected on method: a mascot or figure (round 1), a photographic key visual that has to carry the whole identity (round 2), any poster whose joke needs a caption.

## The system

- **Symbol.** The Klein blue square. It is already the unit of measure and the full stop of the wordmark; it now appears alone, at any scale, always upright and solid (R-COLOR-01, graphic_devices.square). It is never rotated except to show drift.
- **Wordmark.** `cstack` in FreeSans Bold, lowercase, with the full stop drawn as the square, in Klein blue on light grounds and white on dark ones. Never cropped (R-MARK-01).
- **Grid.** 1200 x 640 for README and docs, 1200 x 1200 for a social card. Twelve columns, margin 48, flush left.
- **Type.** Three sizes: display 300, head 64, text 44. 44 is the smallest run that reads at a 324 px phone column (R-TYPE-02).
- **Colour.** Warm white `#F6F4EF`, ink `#151515`, Klein blue `#002FA7`, grey only for a secondary line. A black band when a poster needs a header or footer.
- **Pictures.** When a poster takes a photograph, the photograph holds the square and nothing else (one cube, warm white studio), and sits in a slot the grid gives it; the type never sits on the picture.

## Critique

| Criterion | Reading |
|---|---|
| Brand fit | One colour, one face, the square as the only mark, flush left on the twelve-column grid; the wordmark untouched. |
| Product truth | Every copy line is already in the README or the nine layers; 04 counts the real skills by stage. |
| Distinctiveness | The full stop as the symbol is ownable and costs nothing; the "square larger than the poster" move is the one most likely to be remembered. |
| Risk | Posters 04 and 05 are close to the diagrams they sit beside; if they feel redundant, keep the diagrams and drop the posters. 06 is empty until a photograph that meets the owner's bar exists. |
| Generic moves named | Gradient, sparkle, a 3D render of the square, a square with a face. None used. |
