NEVADA

How far does cstack get without a marketing expert in the room? One founder line ("a sparkling tea in cans") became a brief, a pack, a photoshoot, a set of ads with tests, a website and a map of its own process, in one day, for about US$3.91 of image spend. Each step says whether cstack led or followed, and what it got wrong.

Founder first

followed

The first attempt jumped to pictures, and the founder stopped it ("you never interrogated me as a builder"). Now /brief interviews the founder before anything is made. The make gate stays shut until the founder approves the brief, and closes again on every pivot.

"a better healthier version of the classic value iced tea … a mass hype brand but premium pricing because of premium ingredients … never too sweet"

2

Then cstack pushes back

led

Before making anything downstream, cstack wrote a challenge memo: what it would do better than asked, with evidence.

  • "Never too sweet" becomes a system: a sweetness meter (NOT SWEET · BARELY · A LITTLE) on every can, ad and page. It never passes "a little".
  • Claims kept legal: "low sugar" isn't an FDA-defined claim, and "0 g sugar" needs "not a low calorie food" beside it. Both came off the fronts (21 CFR 101.60).
  • Price tested, not assumed: a fixed price printed on the can, with $3.99 on a tall can tested against $2.99 on 16 oz.
  • Drops with a reason: each flavour drops in its harvest season.
  • Packaging truth: cstack found that the can shape drawn so far holds about 19–21 fl oz, not the 23.5 printed on it. That's an open owner decision.
3

Read the category before the ads

followed (founder asked)then led

A competitor scan of public ad libraries (two leading better-for-you sodas, the best-selling value iced tea, a big tea brand) found the category's conventions:

  • a sugar number as proof;
  • gut and fiber claims;
  • "soda, but better";
  • dessert framing;
  • creator recipes;
  • 25–30% discounts.

Every ad below cites what it copies (one number, seasonal drops told as stories), avoids (gut claims, dessert framing, discounts) or owns (Never too sweet + the meter, a price that never changes, tea sold as the drink).

Identity and packaging, with real craft

led

Colour and type tokens, a NEVER TOO SWEET seal and the meter as vectors, pack v3 with the key line in the top half, and a shelf test. Only the poster pictures were generated; the type is set, not drawn. Spend: $0.

Left: the seal and the meter at sizes down to 32 px. Right: a shelf mock with neutral placeholder competitors. At 3 m the meter can't be read yet, which is logged.

5

A 10-shot campaign, with labels kept true

led

The shoot ran as a sequence (ICON, WORLD, HUMAN, RITUAL, PRODUCT, DETAIL, CULTURE, WEIRD, PROOF, CLOSER) and as Grid → Pick → Polish: cheap grids first, picks with reasons, then polish.

The image model got the can's label wrong in 4 of 7 frames ("22.5 FL OZ", "$3.98"). So no model-drawn label was kept: every can wears the exact label pasted from the 3D render, with the scene's light carried over.

Six of ten frames. The independent review said "fix first": too much yuzu, a calm wellness look rather than mass hype, a weak PRODUCT frame. Those notes are recorded, not hidden.

6

Ads: three bets, each with a test

led

Each bet has a tension, an audience, a hook family and one controlled experiment. Type is set in HTML from the brand tokens, never drawn by a model.

No video clip was made. No video route was priced, and a video model would redraw the label, which this brand forbids.

7

The website, built from the tokens

led

A one-page site with the 3D can as the hero (drag to turn, exact artwork), the meter, the drops with a harvest calendar, and where to buy. It passed cstack's type, token and accessibility checks at 375, 768 and 1440 px. An independent review first said fix (dead buy buttons, a false FAQ line), then promote.

9

Where it still needs a human

  • Taste and register: reviewers rated the campaign "calm premium wellness", while the founder asked for "mass hype". Only the owner can settle that.
  • Product facts: can size, origins, a sensory panel for the meter, and supplier proof for "real yuzu".
  • The test plan: two "ALWAYS" prices can't run in public at once. It needs a regional split.
  • cstack's own gaps, all logged as findings: no flow for process figures; a text check that can't take the expected words; nothing flags work made from files that changed under it; a model router that still ranks a model the owner banned.
